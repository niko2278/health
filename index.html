How it works

Discount cards use prices negotiated with pharmacies. You show the card instead of (or in comparison with) your insurance and pay the lower price.

Pick a card

Choose one of the free cards below. You can get it on your phone, print it, or both.

Show it at the pharmacy

Give the card numbers to the pharmacist when you pick up your prescription.

Pay the lower price

The pharmacist applies the discounted price. Ask them to compare it with your insurance copay.

Get a free discount card

Both cards are free. Prices differ by drug and pharmacy, so it's worth checking both before you pay.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The cards are free to get and free to use. The card providers are paid by pharmacies and partners, not by you.

Is a discount card insurance?

No. It's a discount program, not insurance. It doesn't cover the cost of your medication; it can lower the price you pay at the counter.

How much will I save?

It depends on the medication, the dose and the pharmacy. Some drugs are much cheaper with a card, others barely change. Checking the price before you pay is the best way to know.

Can I use it if I have insurance or Medicare?

You can usually use a card instead of your insurance when its price is lower, but not both on the same prescription. Payments made with a discount card may not count toward your insurance deductible.

Do you sell my information?

No. This site doesn't ask for your name, email or health information. See our privacy policy.
